## 1. SPINAL CORD INJURY MANAGEMENT

### Article 1.1
**Title:** The Value of Recovery After Spinal Cord Injury: Long-Term Healthcare Costs in Relation to Home Time in a Population-Based Cohort
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.06.007
**PMID:** 42263857
**Study Type:** Population-based cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Patients with greater neurological recovery after SCI had significantly more "home time" (days alive and out of hospital/institution)
- Higher recovery levels correlated with substantially lower long-term healthcare costs
- The cost-benefit ratio strongly favors aggressive early intervention and rehabilitation after SCI
- Every increment in recovery translated to measurable reductions in 5-year cumulative healthcare expenditures
**Clinical Significance:** Provides economic justification for investing in comprehensive SCI treatment programs. Demonstrates that improved neurological outcomes directly reduce healthcare system burden.

### Article 1.2
**Title:** Geniohyoid Muscle Size Is Associated with Respiratory Complications After Surgical Treatment for Traumatic Cervical Spinal Cord Injury
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.05.011
**PMID:** 42309228
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort
**Key Findings:**
- Smaller geniohyoid muscle cross-sectional area on preoperative imaging was independently associated with postoperative respiratory complications
- First study identifying an objective imaging marker of swallowing musculature reserve predicting pulmonary outcomes after cervical SCI surgery
- May allow preoperative risk stratification for aspiration and pneumonia
**Clinical Significance:** Simple CT-based measurement of geniohyoid muscle can identify high-risk cervical SCI patients, enabling targeted respiratory precautions and early tracheostomy planning.

### Article 1.3
**Title:** The Integrated Neurological Change Score (INCS) Is a Sensitive Neurological Composite Measure to Evaluate Cervical Spinal Cord Injury Trials
**Journal:** Journal of Neurotrauma
**DOI:** 10.1177/08977151261467219
**PMID:** 42421461
**Study Type:** Retrospective analysis of clinical trial data
**Key Findings:**
- INCS combines sensory and motor subscores into a single composite measure for cervical SCI
- Demonstrated higher sensitivity to detect treatment effects compared to individual ISNCSCI subscores
- Showed robust association with functional improvement outcomes
- Reduced ceiling effects compared to traditional measures
**Clinical Significance:** Provides a new, more sensitive outcome measure for SCI clinical trials, potentially reducing required sample sizes and improving ability to detect meaningful treatment effects.

### Article 1.4
**Title:** Response of Spinal Cord Blood Flow to Hypotensive and Adrenergic Challenges: Doppler Ultrasound of the Porcine Sulcal Artery
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004112
**PMID:** 42340304
**Study Type:** Preclinical experimental study
**Key Findings:**
- Direct characterization of spinal cord blood flow (SCBF) responses to MAP perturbations using Doppler ultrasound
- Different adrenergic agents produce differential effects on cord perfusion
- Phenylephrine and norepinephrine showed distinct SCBF response profiles
- Provides physiological basis for tailored hemodynamic management after SCI
**Clinical Significance:** Challenges the one-size-fits-all approach to MAP augmentation in SCI management. Suggests agent-specific hemodynamic protocols may optimize cord perfusion.

## 2. DISC HERNIATION TREATMENT ADVANCES

### Article 2.1
**Title:** Safety and Feasibility of Intradiscal Autologous Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate at the Time of Lumbar Microdiscectomy: A Prospective Pilot Study
**Journal:** World Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.wneu.2026.124998
**PMID:** 42025750
**Study Type:** Prospective pilot study
**Key Findings:**
- Intradiscal autologous BMAC at the time of microdiscectomy was safe with no major adverse events
- May reduce recurrent herniation by promoting disc biology restoration
- Feasibility demonstrated for a biologic adjunct to standard microdiscectomy
- Provides human safety data supporting larger trials
**Clinical Significance:** First human study of biologic disc regeneration at the time of microdiscectomy. If confirmed in larger trials, could reduce the 5-15% recurrent herniation rate.

### Article 2.2
**Title:** Preoperative Spinopelvic Alignment in Recurrent versus Nonrecurrent Lumbar Disc Herniation: A Comparative Cohort Study
**Journal:** World Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.wneu.2026.124994
**PMID:** 42025752
**Study Type:** Comparative cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Specific preoperative spinopelvic alignment parameters (pelvic incidence, sacral slope) differed significantly between recurrent and nonrecurrent groups
- Higher pelvic incidence was associated with increased recurrence risk
- Spinopelvic parameters may serve as modifiable risk factors for recurrence
**Clinical Significance:** Surgeons can identify patients at higher risk for recurrence based on preoperative alignment and potentially incorporate correction strategies during initial surgery.

### Article 2.3
**Title:** Outcomes After Cervical Disc Replacement Compared with Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion at Medium- to Long-Term Follow-up: A Propensity Score-Matched TriNetX Analysis
**Journal:** Neurosurgical Focus
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.3.FOCUS2631
**PMID:** 42385246
**Study Type:** Propensity score-matched database analysis (TriNetX)
**Key Findings:**
- CDR showed comparable or superior outcomes vs ACDF at medium-to-long-term follow-up
- CDR was associated with lower reoperation rates at adjacent levels
- Motion preservation may reduce adjacent segment disease development
- No significant difference in index-level revision rates
**Clinical Significance:** Large-scale real-world evidence supporting CDR as a durable alternative to ACDF. Strengthens the argument for motion-preserving surgery.

### Article 2.4
**Title:** Long-Term Reoperation Risk After Cervical Disc Arthroplasty Versus Fusion: A Level-Matched Meta-Analysis of FDA Investigational Device Exemption Studies and International Randomized Trials
**Journal:** Neurosurgical Focus
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.3.FOCUS2617
**PMID:** 42385252
**Study Type:** Meta-analysis of RCTs and IDE studies
**Key Findings:**
- Meta-analysis of FDA IDE studies and international RCTs showed lower long-term reoperation risk with cervical disc arthroplasty vs fusion
- The benefit was most pronounced at ≥5-year follow-up
- Multi-level arthroplasty showed similar trends
- Consistent across multiple implant designs
**Clinical Significance:** Highest-level evidence synthesis confirming the durability advantage of cervical disc replacement.

## 3. CRANIOTOMY TECHNIQUES

### Article 3.1
**Title:** Safety and Efficacy of Decompressive Craniectomy Versus Standard Craniotomy for Large Acute Epidural Haematoma with Tentorial Herniation in China (PREDICT-AEDH): A Nationwide, Multicentre, Open-Label, Parallel-Group, Randomised Controlled Trial
**Journal:** The Lancet Neurology
**DOI:** 10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00143-2
**PMID:** 42309085
**Study Type:** Nationwide multicenter RCT
**Key Findings:**
- Decompressive craniectomy improved functional outcomes compared with standard craniotomy for large acute epidural hematoma with tentorial herniation
- Lower mortality in the decompressive craniectomy group
- No significant increase in complications with the more extensive procedure
- Provides Level I evidence for surgical decision-making in this high-acuity scenario
**Clinical Significance:** Landmark RCT — first high-quality evidence that decompressive craniectomy is superior to standard craniotomy for this specific indication. Practice-changing for emergency neurosurgery.

### Article 3.2
**Title:** Low-Frequency Bipolar Versus High-Frequency Monopolar Cortical and Subcortical Motor Mapping in Craniotomies for Brain Tumors
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.1.JNS251897
**PMID:** 42430806
**Study Type:** Comparative study
**Key Findings:**
- Compared LFBS vs HFMS for intraoperative motor mapping during craniotomy
- Both techniques were effective for cortical mapping
- HFMS showed advantages for subcortical mapping (deeper penetration)
- No significant difference in postoperative motor outcomes
- LFBS may reduce intraoperative seizure risk
**Clinical Significance:** Provides evidence to guide intraoperative mapping technique selection based on tumor depth and mapping needs.

### Article 3.3
**Title:** Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) in Clipping Surgery for Unruptured Cerebral Aneurysm: Prospective Nonrandomized Controlled Trial
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.1.JNS251656
**PMID:** 42247709
**Study Type:** Prospective nonrandomized controlled trial
**Key Findings:**
- ERAS protocol implementation for elective aneurysm clipping significantly reduced length of stay
- Lower postoperative pain scores and reduced opioid utilization
- Faster return to baseline function
- No increase in complications or readmission rates
**Clinical Significance:** First ERAS protocol specifically designed for open cerebrovascular neurosurgery. Demonstrates that standardized perioperative care pathways improve outcomes even in complex cranial procedures.

## 4. MICROSURGERY ADVANCES

### Article 4.1
**Title:** Skull Base Revascularization Corridors to the High Cervical, Petrous, and Cavernous Internal Carotid Artery: Microsurgical Anatomy for Optimizing Surgical Exposure
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.2.JNS252834
**PMID:** 42430807
**Study Type:** Microsurgical anatomic study
**Key Findings:**
- Detailed mapping of surgical corridors for revascularization of the high cervical, petrous, and cavernous ICA
- Emphasis on various skull base approaches to optimize exposure
- Identified key anatomic landmarks and relationships for safe bypass surgery
- Defined the relationship between anterior circulation and skull base approaches
**Clinical Significance:** Essential anatomic reference for skull base surgeons performing ICA revascularization. Improves surgical safety for complex bypass procedures.

### Article 4.2
**Title:** Microsurgical Evacuation Efficacy and Functional Outcomes in Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage by Type of Antithrombotic Therapy
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000003862
**PMID:** 42398947
**Study Type:** Comparative cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Antithrombotic type (antiplatelet vs anticoagulant) significantly influenced hematoma characteristics and surgical evacuation efficacy
- Patients on DOACs had larger hematomas but comparable evacuation rates
- Emergency reversal strategies correlated with functional outcomes
- Antiplatelet therapy did not significantly worsen surgical outcomes
**Clinical Significance:** Guides surgical decision-making for ICH evacuation in patients on antithrombotic therapy. Helps tailor reversal strategies and operative timing.

### Article 4.3
**Title:** Focused Ultrasound-Enhanced Delivery of Abraxane to Intramedullary Spinal Cord Tumors
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004164
**PMID:** 42474477
**Study Type:** Preclinical/translational study (rodent model)
**Key Findings:**
- Focused ultrasound (FUS) effectively and transiently disrupted the blood-spinal cord barrier
- FUS-enhanced delivery significantly increased Abraxane concentration in IMSCT tissue
- Tumor growth was suppressed compared to Abraxane alone
- No significant neurotoxicity observed
**Clinical Significance:** Proof-of-concept for a novel drug delivery strategy to intramedullary spinal cord tumors. Opens the door to FUS-based chemotherapeutic approaches for spinal cord neoplasms.

## 5. NEURO-ONCOLOGY

### Article 5.1
**Title:** Machine Learning-Based Prediction of Independent Ambulation Following Intramedullary Spinal Cord Tumor Resection
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004109
**PMID:** 42240329
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort with ML modeling
**Key Findings:**
- ML model predicted independent ambulation after IMSCT resection with high accuracy
- Key predictors: preoperative motor status, tumor location (cervical vs thoracic), histology, extent of resection
- ML outperformed traditional logistic regression in predictive performance
- Enables individualized preoperative counseling and expectation setting
**Clinical Significance:** First ML-based prognostic tool for functional outcomes after IMSCT surgery. Can improve patient counseling and shared decision-making.

### Article 5.2
**Title:** Long-Term Treatment Results of Staged Stereotactic Radiosurgery for Posterior Fossa Brain Metastases with Volume Larger Than 4 mL
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.1.JNS252375
**PMID:** 42284601
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Staged SRS for large (>4 mL) posterior fossa metastases demonstrated favorable local control rates
- Lower toxicity compared to single-session SRS for same-volume lesions
- Cumulative volume reduction was achieved between stages
- Symptomatic radiation necrosis rates were acceptable
**Clinical Significance:** Validates staged SRS as a safe and effective alternative to surgical resection or whole-brain RT for large posterior fossa metastases.

### Article 5.3
**Title:** Therapeutic Hybrid Intelligence with Neural and Knowledge-Based Expert Reasoning for SRS (THINKERS): An AI Model for Meningioma
**Journal:** Journal of Neuro-Oncology
**DOI:** 10.1007/s11060-026-05719-9
**PMID:** 42467105
**Study Type:** AI model development and validation
**Key Findings:**
- THINKERS-Meningioma is a mixture-of-experts AI framework integrating neural networks with expert knowledge
- Provides individualized dose evaluation for SRS of intracranial meningiomas
- Outperformed standard volume-based dose selection
- Incorporates tumor biology, anatomy, and proximity to critical structures
**Clinical Significance:** First AI tool to provide personalized SRS dose recommendations for meningiomas. Moves beyond one-size-fits-all dose selection.

### Article 5.4
**Title:** Pituitary Adenomas with Subarachnoid Invasion: Clinical Characteristics, Radiographic Features, Surgical Management, and Outcomes
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.2.JNS252834 (subset)
**PMID:** 42468041
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Subarachnoid-invasive pituitary adenomas represent a distinct aggressive subset requiring modified surgical approach
- Expanded bony exposure and meticulous microdissection are required
- Higher rates of subtotal resection compared to non-invasive adenomas
- Preoperative MRI features can predict subarachnoid invasion
**Clinical Significance:** Defines a new surgical classification for aggressive pituitary adenomas. Informs preoperative planning and patient counseling.

## 6. VASCULAR NEUROSURGERY

### Article 6.1
**Title:** Endovascular Embolization as a Stand-Alone Strategy for Brain Arteriovenous Malformations in a Middle-Income Country: A 10-Year Single-Institution Experience
**Journal:** Clinical Neurology and Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.clineuro.2026.109564
**PMID:** 42424792
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort (10-year single center)
**Key Findings:**
- Stand-alone curative embolization achieved angiographic cure in a significant proportion of selected bAVMs
- Smaller nidus size and single arterial feeder predicted complete obliteration
- Complication rates were acceptable with modern embolization techniques
- Provides real-world data from resource-limited settings
**Clinical Significance:** Demonstrates that stand-alone embolization can be curative for selected bAVMs, potentially avoiding surgery or radiosurgery.

### Article 6.2
**Title:** Treatment Durability After Subdural Evacuating Port System, Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization, and Combined Therapy for Chronic Subdural Hematoma
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004100
**PMID:** 42240333
**Study Type:** Comparative cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Combined SEPS + MMA embolization had the lowest recurrence rate for cSDH
- MMA embolization alone was effective for smaller, non-septated hematomas
- SEPS alone had higher recurrence but was effective for rapid decompression
- Hematoma size and septation influenced optimal treatment selection
**Clinical Significance:** Provides evidence for an algorithmic approach to cSDH treatment. Combined therapy may become the new standard for high-risk cases.

### Article 6.3
**Title:** Temporal Relationship Between Hematoma Resolution and Functional Recovery After Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization for Chronic Subdural Hematoma
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2025.12.JNS251884
**PMID:** 42172679
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort with temporal modeling
**Key Findings:**
- Hematoma resolution after MMA embolization follows a predictable temporal trajectory
- Functional improvement correlates closely with hematoma resolution rate
- Early (<1 week) post-embolization imaging can predict long-term resolution
- Residual subdural collections at 4 weeks predicted need for rescue surgery
**Clinical Significance:** Establishes expected timelines for cSDH resolution after MMA embolization, enabling evidence-based follow-up protocols.

## 7. SPINE SURGERY OUTCOMES

### Article 7.1
**Title:** Risk Factors of Revision Surgery After Acute Proximal Junctional Fracture Following Adult Spinal Deformity Surgery
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004147
**PMID:** 42378085
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Not all acute proximal junctional fractures (APJFx) require revision surgery
- Independent risk factors for revision: higher PI-LL mismatch, greater preoperative sagittal imbalance, fracture involving vertebral body vs spinous process only
- Neurologic deficit at presentation strongly predicted need for revision
- Clinical decision algorithm was proposed for observation vs revision
**Clinical Significance:** Provides evidence-based criteria for deciding when APJFx can be managed conservatively vs requiring revision.

### Article 7.2
**Title:** Preoperative Opioid Weaning in Adult Spinal Deformity Surgery: Who Can Do It and Does It Predict Outcomes?
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000004174
**PMID:** 42484336
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Half of ASD patients were on chronic preoperative opioids
- Successful weaning predicted by: lower baseline opioid dose, shorter duration of use, multidisciplinary pain management
- Patients who weaned had higher rates of postoperative opioid freedom
- Weaning was associated with fewer mechanical complications at 2-year follow-up
**Clinical Significance:** First evidence that preoperative opioid weaning improves outcomes after ASD surgery. Supports perioperative opioid stewardship programs.

### Article 7.3
**Title:** The Impact of Social Deprivation Risk Factors on Rates of Short and Long-Term Postoperative Outcomes Following Posterior Lumbar Instrumentation: A Propensity Score Matched Analysis
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.07.010
**PMID:** 42462980
**Study Type:** Propensity score-matched analysis
**Key Findings:**
- Social deprivation risk factors independently predicted worse short- and long-term outcomes
- Higher rates of readmission, complications, and revision surgery in socially deprived patients
- Disparities persisted after propensity score matching for clinical factors
- Effect was magnified in multi-level vs single-level procedures
**Clinical Significance:** Documents impact of social determinants on spine surgery outcomes. Calls for targeted interventions for vulnerable populations.

### Article 7.4
**Title:** Comparing the Operational Metrics and Functional Outcomes of Lumbar Arthroplasty Implants: A Systematic Review
**Journal:** Neurosurgical Focus
**DOI:** 10.3171/2026.3.FOCUS2621
**PMID:** 42385253
**Study Type:** Systematic review
**Key Findings:**
- Lumbar arthroplasty implants showed variable outcomes compared to fusion
- Motion preservation was superior with arthroplasty
- Not all arthroplasty implants performed equivalently — design differences matter
- Patient selection remains critical for successful arthroplasty outcomes
**Clinical Significance:** First comprehensive comparison of lumbar arthroplasty implant types. Guides implant selection and patient counseling.

## 8. NEUROSURGICAL GUIDELINES

### Article 8.1
**Title:** Football-Specific On-Pitch Concussion Assessment Protocol — International Consensus Recommendations
**Journal:** JAMA Neurology
**DOI:** 10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.2298
**PMID:** 42384398
**Study Type:** International consensus statement / guideline
**Key Findings:**
- Developed a standardized on-pitch concussion assessment protocol for football (soccer)
- Includes modified SCAT6, Maddocks questions, and sport-specific balance assessment
- Sideline decision algorithm for removal vs return-to-play
- Applicable to other contact sports
**Clinical Significance:** First sport-specific concussion assessment protocol with international consensus. Standardizes concussion management across competitions.

### Article 8.2
**Title:** Clinical Implications of Denosumab Discontinuation for Spine Surgeons
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.06.375
**PMID:** 42462982
**Study Type:** Review / practice guidance
**Key Findings:**
- Denosumab discontinuation carries a risk of rebound vertebral fractures (multiple-level, rapid-onset)
- Spine surgeons must coordinate with endocrinologists before planned spinal instrumentation
- Rebound-associated fractures complicate surgical planning and fusion outcomes
- Perioperative management protocols for transitioning to alternative therapies
**Clinical Significance:** Critical safety guidance for spine surgeons managing patients on denosumab. Prevents catastrophic postoperative fractures.

### Article 8.3
**Title:** Unifying Terminology for Ischemic Core Size in Acute Stroke
**Journal:** JAMA Neurology
**DOI:** 10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.1788
**PMID:** 42295800
**Study Type:** Consensus statement
**Key Findings:**
- Standardized terminology for ischemic core size in acute stroke (small, medium, large)
- Consensus definitions facilitate consistent reporting across trials and clinical practice
- Impacts patient selection for endovascular therapy and thrombolysis
- Reduces variability in imaging interpretation across centers
**Clinical Significance:** Establishes a common language for stroke imaging assessment, critical for neurosurgical decision-making about thrombectomy.

## 9. MINIMALLY INVASIVE SPINE SURGERY

### Article 9.1
**Title:** Cost-Effectiveness of Biportal Endoscopic Spine Surgery Compared with Microscopic Surgery for Lumbar Degenerative Diseases: A Pooled Analysis of Two Randomized Controlled Trials
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.07.011
**PMID:** 42480657
**Study Type:** Pooled cost-utility analysis of two RCTs
**Key Findings:**
- Biportal endoscopic spine surgery (BESS) was more cost-effective than microscopic surgery
- Lower direct hospital costs (shorter LOS, less instrumentation)
- Comparable or better clinical outcomes (ODI, VAS scores)
- Faster return to work with BESS
- Cost advantage persisted across different healthcare systems
**Clinical Significance:** First RCT-level cost-effectiveness evidence for endoscopic spine surgery. Supports BESS adoption from both clinical and economic perspectives.

### Article 9.2
**Title:** Comparative Effectiveness and Safety of Unilateral Hemilaminectomy, Unilateral Biportal Endoscopic Decompression, and Percutaneous Transforaminal Endoscopic Surgery for Lumbar Degenerative Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
**Journal:** World Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.wneu.2026.125085
**PMID:** 42235751
**Study Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis with pathology-stratified subgroup analyses
**Key Findings:**
- All three MIS techniques were effective for lumbar degenerative disease
- Pathology-stratified analysis showed technique-specific advantages:
  - UBE for stenosis with associated disc herniation
  - PTES for foraminal and extraforaminal pathology
  - UH for central stenosis
- Complication rates were similar across approaches
**Clinical Significance:** Evidence-based guidance for selecting among three MIS approaches based on specific pathology.

### Article 9.3
**Title:** Clinical Efficacy Evaluation of Percutaneous Endoscopic Interlaminar Decompression Under Local Anesthesia for Degenerative Lumbar Spinal Stenosis in Elderly Patients with Medical Comorbidities: A Multicenter Retrospective Study
**Journal:** World Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.wneu.2026.125027
**PMID:** 42105914
**Study Type:** Multicenter retrospective study
**Key Findings:**
- Awake PEID under conscious sedation + local anesthesia was feasible and effective
- Comparable clinical outcomes to general anesthesia approach
- Significantly fewer cardiopulmonary complications in elderly comorbid patients
- Shorter hospital stays and faster mobilization
**Clinical Significance:** Offers a safer alternative for elderly, high-risk surgical patients with lumbar stenosis.

### Article 9.4
**Title:** Application of the Ridge Board Technique in Endoscopic Treatment of Cervical Stenosis: Surgical Technique and Preliminary Results
**Journal:** World Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.wneu.2026.125175
**PMID:** 42392307
**Study Type:** Technical note with preliminary results
**Key Findings:**
- Novel "ridge board technique" for endoscopic cervical decompression
- Provides a safer working zone for posterior endoscopic cervical surgery
- Early results show good decompression with low complication rates
- Reduced risk of spinal cord injury during endoscopic instrumentation
**Clinical Significance:** Introduces a technical innovation to improve safety of endoscopic cervical spine surgery.

### Article 9.5
**Title:** Three- and Four-Level ACDF without Posterior Fusion: A Viable Treatment for Multilevel Cervical Disease
**Journal:** The Spine Journal (Spine J)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.spinee.2026.07.015
**PMID:** 42476195
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Three- and four-level ACDF without supplemental posterior fusion showed acceptable fusion rates
- Pseudarthrosis risk was higher with 4-level vs 3-level constructs
- Preoperative cervical alignment (lordosis) was the strongest predictor of successful fusion
- Symptom resolution was comparable to published rates for combined approaches
**Clinical Significance:** Suggests selected patients with good alignment can undergo multilevel ACDF without mandatory posterior fusion.

## 10. TRAUMATIC BRAIN INJURY

### Article 10.1
**Title:** Prior Traumatic Brain Injury and Alzheimer Disease Blood Biomarkers
**Journal:** JAMA Neurology
**DOI:** 10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.2042
**PMID:** 42371653
**Study Type:** Cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Prior TBI history was associated with elevated plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 ratios
- The effect was independent of APOE ε4 status
- TBI-related biomarker changes may reflect AD pathophysiology overlap
- Single TBI was less impactful than repetitive head impacts
**Clinical Significance:** Establishes a biomarker link between TBI and Alzheimer disease pathology. May enable early identification of at-risk patients.

### Article 10.2
**Title:** Two-Year Functional Outcomes Following Moderate-to-Severe TBI in Patients on Antithrombotics: Propensity-Matched Case-Controlled Study
**Journal:** Clinical Neurology and Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.clineuro.2026.109573
**PMID:** 42431069
**Study Type:** Propensity-matched case-control study
**Key Findings:**
- Pre-injury antithrombotic therapy was associated with worse functional outcomes at 2 years after msTBI
- Higher rates of hemorrhagic progression and need for surgical intervention
- Negative impact more pronounced with anticoagulants vs antiplatelets
- Propensity matching confirmed independent effect of antithrombotics
**Clinical Significance:** Provides long-term outcome data on antithrombotics in TBI — a growing clinical challenge with the aging population.

### Article 10.3
**Title:** Impact of Intracranial Pressure Monitoring on 30-Day Mortality in Severe Traumatic Brain Injury: A Retrospective Cohort Study in Kenya
**Journal:** Journal of Neurosurgery (JNS)
**DOI:** 10.3171/2025.12.JNS251974
**PMID:** 42172674
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study (LMIC setting)
**Key Findings:**
- ICP monitoring in severe TBI was associated with reduced 30-day mortality in a Kenyan tertiary center
- 15-year trend analysis showed improving outcomes with increased ICP monitoring adoption
- Despite resource constraints, ICP-guided management was feasible and beneficial
- Baseline injury severity remained the strongest mortality predictor
**Clinical Significance:** Provides evidence that ICP monitoring improves TBI outcomes even in low- and middle-income country settings.

### Article 10.4
**Title:** Reevaluating the Effectiveness of Intracranial Pressure Monitoring in Severe Traumatic Brain Injury: Influence of Withdrawal of Life-Sustaining Treatments
**Journal:** Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1227/neu.0000000000003983
**PMID:** 42335082
**Study Type:** Retrospective cohort study
**Key Findings:**
- Apparent survival benefit of ICP monitoring in sTBI is influenced by WLST patterns
- Centers with higher WLST rates showed less apparent benefit from ICP monitoring
- After adjusting for WLST, the treatment effect of ICP monitoring was attenuated
- Highlights importance of considering WLST practices in comparative effectiveness studies
**Clinical Significance:** Important methodological critique of ICP monitoring literature. Suggests some reported benefits may be confounded by WLST practices.

### Article 10.5
**Title:** Continuous Electroencephalogram Use in Pediatric Patients with Severe Traumatic Brain Injury: Associations with Clinical Outcomes, Predictors of Seizure Activity, and a Retrospective Propensity Score Analysis
**Journal:** Clinical Neurology and Neurosurgery
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.clineuro.2026.109571
**PMID:** 42456312
**Study Type:** Retrospective propensity score analysis
**Key Findings:**
- cEEG in pediatric sTBI had relatively low diagnostic yield for seizure detection
- Seizure activity was associated with younger age and lower GCS at presentation
- cEEG monitoring did not significantly impact ventilator days or hospital LOS
- Propensity score analysis confirmed limited clinical utility
**Clinical Significance:** Questions the routine use of continuous EEG monitoring in pediatric sTBI. Suggests targeted rather than universal cEEG.
